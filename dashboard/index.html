Which NFL positions are worth paying for?

Quarterback spending has the clearest payoff, +0.31 points per game for each point of cap share, almost all of it through the passing game. Receivers, linemen, edge rushers and cornerbacks pay off on the side of the ball where they play. Tight end looks valuable too, but it fails a basic placebo check, so I treat it as noise. All of it together explains about 10% of how teams perform.

Salary-cap spending by position against team results, 2013–2025: 416 team-seasons, 32 teams. Starters are separated from bench depth using snap counts. QB returns taper above roughly 14% of the cap (about $30M+ today), though the data cannot say whether they turn negative.

How to read this

Data. Per-player cap hits (Over the Cap), snap counts, results and EPA/play from nflverse, 2013–2025. The cap data covers part of the official cap, so everything is expressed as shares.

Starter means the top-N players at a position by snaps (QB1, RB1, WR3, TE1, OL5, DL2, EDGE2, LB2, CB3, S2). Everyone else is bench.

Caution. These are associations, not causal effects. Ten positions across several outcomes means a few significant results are expected by chance. The full write-up, with all checks, is in the project notebook.

Cap share
A position group’s cap hits divided by the team’s total tracked cap hits that season. One point is about $1.6M for an average team.
EPA (expected points added)
How many points a play added compared with what that situation usually yields. Here it is EPA per 100 plays, and defense is flipped so higher is better for both sides.
Estimate and 95% interval
The dot is the best estimate. The line is the range of values the data cannot rule out.
Significant at 5%
The interval excludes zero, so a true effect of zero is unlikely. With ten positions and several outcomes, a few results clear this bar by chance.
Significant under k of 6 starter rules
“Starter” was defined six different ways. The number counts how many definitions keep the result significant.
Placebo check
Spending on offensive positions should not move the defense, and spending on defensive positions should not move the offense. A position that does fails the check, and its result is probably noise.
Data: nflverse, including Over the Cap contract data, 2013–2025. Every number on this page comes from the same models as the project notebook.
